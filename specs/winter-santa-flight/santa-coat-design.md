# Santa coat design

## Evidence and decision

The existing seated preview shows an evenly inflated coat and sleeves, with a pink cast in the illuminated cloth. The present 26 coat rings linearly subdivide six profile anchors, so subdivision preserves the original profile corners. The 24-sided torso and 16-sided sleeves also leave too few samples for restrained fabric folds. Recomputed normals do not join the duplicated UV seam vertices.

A diagnostic render used the actual shipped models, existing night lights, RoomEnvironment intensity 0.38, ACES tone mapping, and exposure 1.22. It compared the current material with three alternatives; no scene lighting changed. The contact sheet is `test-results/santa-coat-calibration.png`, ordered baseline, rich, warm, deep from left to right and top to bottom. All four views rendered without page errors. The unchanged full team measured 141,707 triangles, 54 draw calls, 31 geometries, and 19 textures.

| Variant       | Color      | Bump scale | Assessment                                                      |
| ------------- | ---------- | ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Baseline      | `0xb51b23` | 0.025      | Too pink and uniformly bright on shoulders and sleeves.         |
| Rich          | `0x980d12` | 0.008      | Rich red, but darker than necessary.                            |
| Selected warm | `0xa20b0e` | 0.008      | Clear classic red, with useful shaded shape and a matching cap. |
| Deep          | `0x87090e` | 0.008      | Unnecessarily dark for the intended friendly character.         |

Keep `MeshStandardMaterial`, roughness 1, metalness 0, environment intensity 0.1, the existing owned 256-pixel wool map and its repeat of 4. Set the one shared red material to the selected color and bump scale. Do not add sheen, clearcoat, a new roughness map, emissive color, or a downloaded texture. The cloth silhouette and folds provide the needed detail; increasing noisy bump relief would obscure them. The exact same material instance must remain on cap, coat, sleeves, and trousers. Keep the existing shared white fur material unchanged.

## Geometry recipe

Retain the existing six coat profile anchors, anatomical dimensions, seated placement, and attachment hierarchy. Replace linear interpolation with uniform Catmull-Rom interpolation, tension 0.5, sampled at 49 rings. Two matching curves can encode center `(x, y, z)` and profile `(radiusX, y, radiusZ)`; both must use the same uniform parameter so their height stays aligned. Require increasing ring height and positive radii. Use 48 angular segments, retaining the duplicated UV seam.

Build folds into the existing coat position buffer before calculating normals. Use angle zero at the front (+X), `a = atan2(sin(angle), cos(angle))`, front mask `max(0, cos(angle))`, side mask `sin(angle)^2`, and smooth height windows that vanish at their boundaries. Radial displacement follows `(cos(angle), 0, sin(angle))`. Retain the profile dimensions; limit the total displacement to 0.028 model units.

- Above the belt, use two shallow compression ridges centered at heights 0.43 and 0.49, widths 0.035 and 0.045, amplitudes 0.016 and 0.009. Give each a neighboring trough about 0.035 higher, with roughly half the ridge amplitude. Weight these mainly toward the front and sides, so the rear remains quiet.
- Between chest and underarm, use a broad diagonal tension ridge with center height `0.77 - 0.18 * abs(sin(angle))`, width 0.045, amplitude 0.014. Add a trough 0.045 below it with amplitude -0.009. Weight toward the front and sides and taper out below height 0.48 and above 0.91. This produces folds converging toward the sleeve rather than horizontal rings around the entire body.
- Below the belt, retain only small asymmetrical seated compression: amplitude at most 0.012, within heights 0.10 to 0.29, primarily toward the front. Most of this area is behind the sleigh, so do not spend geometry on additional hidden details.
- Add a recognizable white-fur front opening from height 0.35 to 1.01, centered at z = 0. Use the exact shared white material. Follow the actual folded front surface by interpolating adjacent dense coat rings. Sweep a shallow convex ellipse with x half-depth 0.018 and z half-width 0.045, using 25 longitudinal sections and 16 angular segments. Pinch both endpoints closed and taper the neighboring sections. Below height 0.43, use `press=smoothstep(0.35,0.43,y)`: center x is `front+0.006-0.02*(1-press)`, x half-depth is `0.018*taper*press`, and z half-width is `0.045*taper`. This tucks the lower end behind the belt so the opening does not read as a pointed necktie. Place the strip in a named `santa-coat-details` Group under `upperContents`, so rigid batching preserves the existing named coat and collar. Replace the exposed deep torus buckle with one rounded rectangular brass frame: outer contour 0.176 by 0.126, inner contour 0.14 by 0.09, corner radii 0.01 and 0.006, extrusion depth 0.012, bevel size/thickness 0.002 and one bevel segment. Its final bounds are 0.18 wide, 0.13 high and 0.016 deep, including bevels. Keep the existing brass material and named buckle, with center x equal to the actual coat front at height 0.35 plus 0.016. These adjustments follow actual rendered front/profile inspection and leave the belt and other garment geometry unchanged. The strip costs about 800 triangles and one draw call. Omit buttons: the beard hides the upper opening, and the existing buckle provides the visible hardware. Do not also add the earlier red placket ridge; the fur edge now defines the construction seam.

Use Gaussian profiles `exp(-pow(distance / width, 2))` for the listed ridges and troughs. Avoid a uniform sine wave around the torso. A very small side-dependent phase or center-height offset, at most 0.012, can keep the two sides from appearing stamped from the same mold.

Keep the sleeve centerline and shoulder/wrist positions. Increase each sleeve to 40 longitudinal intervals and 24 angular segments. Replace its circumferential corrugation with two restrained inner-elbow compression creases at normalized positions 0.57 and 0.68, Gaussian width 0.04, and amplitudes 0.014 and 0.012 model units. Put a trough 0.035 after each ridge at half amplitude. With the current sweep frame, `max(0, -cos(angle))^2` selects the upper/inside elbow surface; fade the effect in over normalized positions 0.43 to 0.49 and out over 0.76 to 0.82. The outside elbow should stay broad and rounded. The selected wrist-motion delta and GPU morph must continue to move the complete sleeve end with the hand group.

After computing normals, average and normalize the first and last angular normals in each procedural ring, writing the same result to both UV-seam vertices. Apply this only to the coat and procedural sleeves. Sleeve neutral and deformed morph normal calculations must apply the same seam rule before deriving normal deltas. Do not recompute, weld, or otherwise alter the donor head or cap normals. This can be a small typed helper accepting geometry and angular segment count; no general mesh-processing abstraction is needed.

Keep the collar silhouette and shared trim unchanged initially. If the final close view still reveals a polygonal collar rim, increase that existing loft from 20 to 32 angular segments and apply the same seam-normal rule; do not add fur cards, shell layers, or another texture.

## Scope and implementation checks

The product change belongs in `santaModel.ts`; the matching material expectations and geometry/morph checks belong in `santaModel.test.ts`. Existing renderer, asset loading, source GLBs, formation, deer, lights, page lifecycle, and routes stay outside this change. Update the shared-color assertion while preserving identity assertions for cap/coat and cap-fur/collar. Preserve resource ownership, idempotent disposal, and stable per-frame buffers.

With 49-by-49 coat vertices and two 41-by-25 sleeves, the added topology is roughly 5,500 triangles, leaving the full scene near 147,000 triangles before small collar adjustments. Use the actual rendered result as the final count; remain below 180,000 triangles and 125 draw calls. The front strip adds about 800 more triangles and one draw call, leaving the projected scene near 148,000 triangles and 55 draw calls. No new material or texture is needed and no geometry should be rebuilt during animation.

Final verification should include: monotonic profile heights and finite positive radii; finite geometry and morph values; matching seam normals; source-buffer preservation; unchanged shared material identity; wrist/cuff/rein contact at the motion extrema; and existing ownership/disposal tests. Capture seated front three-quarter and profile views at neutral and pull extrema under the unchanged production lighting, plus an actual distant flight frame. Reject visible tube rings, sharp profile bands, a front normal seam, shiny cloth, or a cap/coat color mismatch. The color choice is directly render-calibrated; the proposed new topology and fold placement still require implementation and those final visual checks.
